## Process Execution Benchmarking

Abb et al. (2025) explore process execution benchmarking as a way to identify opportunities for process improvement. Their approach compares an organisation’s event log with a benchmark process to identify possible activity changes based on behavioural similarity. It also considers whether those changes are practical and estimates how they may affect overall process performance. This highlights how process analysis can provide useful evidence to support better decisions about process improvement (Abb et al. 2025, p. 19).
I chose this artefact because it helped me develop a clearer understanding of process analysis. At first I thought process analysis was mainly about identifying problems in an existing process. After exploring this concept I realised that good process analysis goes further. It should help explain what can be improved why a particular change makes sense and how that change could influence performance. This artefact demonstrates my learning because I can now see how process data can be used to guide redesign decisions. It is meaningful evidence of my understanding of BPM because it shows how analysis can support more informed and effective process improvement.

## Prioritising High-Impact Processes

Lubane and Kirikova (2025) discuss how organisations can decide which business processes should be improved first when time and resources are limited. Their approach looks at factors such as process health, strategic importance, feasibility and the relationships between different processes. The study also points out that improving one process can have an effect on other connected processes. This suggests that process analysis should be approached strategically. Instead of treating every process equally organisations should focus on the processes that provide the most value or have the greatest impact on the organisation (Lubane & Kirikova 2025, p. 36).
I selected this artefact because it helped me better understand how process prioritisation works in practice. Before studying this topic I assumed that the most inefficient process should always be analysed first. I now understand that organisations also need to consider strategic value, available resources and the possible impact on other areas of the business. This artefact demonstrates my learning because I can now view process analysis as an organisation-wide activity where different processes are often connected. It is meaningful evidence of my BPM understanding because it shows that choosing the right process to analyse is an important step before any improvement work begins.

## Workarounds and Real Process Execution

Bartelheimer et al. (2025) explore how employees sometimes use workarounds when standard business processes do not fit the practical situation they are dealing with. Their study looks at 13 workarounds across four organisations and finds differences between formal process models, event logs and what employees actually do in practice. This shows that system records may not always give a complete picture of how a process is really carried out. As a result the quality of the data can be affected and this may reduce the accuracy of process analysis findings (Bartelheimer et al. 2025, p. 339).
This is the reason why I have chosen this artefact, as it taught me that process analysis does not necessarily have to rely on data available in the system or processes documented in process manuals. I realized that techniques like observation and interviews, along with employee feedback, can be used to identify the activities that are not present in the recorded data. This artefact demonstrates my learning because it shows how human behaviour can directly influence process performance. It is meaningful evidence of my BPM understanding because good process analysis requires accurate data as well as a clear understanding of how the process actually works in real situations.

## Process Mining and Bottleneck Analysis

Lee, Shin & Lee (2025) describe the process mining approach to manufacturing processes analysis through process, time, resource, and quality aspects. This study reveals the application of event log data in the identification of the process bottlenecks and workloads, and the need for process improvement. Process mining is a way to gain an understanding of real process performance using data analysis instead of relying solely on observation and assumptions (Lee, Shin & Lee 2025, p. 5641).
I selected this artefact because it gave me a more practical understanding of process mining. Before studying this topic I mainly associated process analysis with observation and manual investigation. I now understand that event data can reveal waiting times, resource issues and bottlenecks that may be difficult to detect manually. This artefact demonstrates my learning because it connects technology with process performance. It is meaningful evidence of my BPM understanding because process mining can support more accurate analysis and continuous process improvement.
